Legend. **S** = physically supported (matches primary source or measurement). **A** = convenient approximation (plausible but unmeasured, or known to differ from the rig). **U** = could not be verified. Every link is also listed with a one-line note in `sources.md`.

Rig facts used throughout (Exp 6, `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`): PCM1808 at 44.1 kHz (code default 48 kHz, device rate wins); laser channel raw RMS 0.25–0.43 FS when quiet; 200–4000 Hz RMS 0.012–0.015 quiet vs 0.021–0.031 speaking; standard mic 0.003 quiet vs 0.004–0.05 speaking.

Derived rig numbers (computed here):
- In-band SNR = 10·log10((RMS_speak² − RMS_quiet²)/RMS_quiet²) = **3.1 to 7.5 dB, central ≈ 4.5 dB** (amplitude ratio 1.75–2.6×). The "SNR ≈ 2" in Exp 6 is the *(signal+noise)/noise* amplitude ratio, i.e. 6 dB of total-to-floor, which is 4.8 dB of true SNR.
- Hum vs in-band speech: raw 0.34 vs in-band speaking 0.026 → hum is **≈ 22 dB above** the in-band speech signal; by power the hum share is 99.4 %, the "96 %" in Exp 6 is the amplitude share (1 − 0.013/0.34).
- Upper bound on the hum that leaks into 200–3800 Hz on the rig: (0.013/0.34)² = **≤ 0.15 % of hum power** (that figure also contains all electronic noise, so the true harmonic leak is smaller).

## 1. Surface vibration (simulator stage 1)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A sound-driven thin surface is a sum of 1–3 damped mass-spring (2nd-order) modes, X/P = w²/(s²+(w/Q)s+w²). | **A** (LTI is supported, the mode count/placement is not) | Davis et al., *The Visual Microphone* (SIGGRAPH 2014) model the object response as LTI, measure displacements "in the order of a micrometer" at 57–95 dB SPL, "approximately linear in Pascals", and find "most objects have a stronger response at lower frequencies" with non-monotonic roll-off attributed to "the excitement of vibration modes" [[VisualMic](https://people.csail.mit.edu/mrub/papers/VisualMic_SIGGRAPH2014.pdf)]. | Keep the LTI resonator form (good). Replace the random modes by a *measured* |H(f)| as soon as 5+ paired std/laser clips exist (`--calibrate-from` already does this) — but band-pass both channels first (see §4). |
| Mode frequencies uniform 250–3000 Hz, Q 0.7–10. | **A** — plausible for small light targets, not for window glass | Window glass: modal test of a 1480×1230 mm double-glazed window found **29 modes between 30 and ~200 Hz**, seven significant modes between 94 and 136 Hz, damping 1.8–3.2 % (Q ≈ 15–28) [[Pietrzko, IOA](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/sj_pietrzko_vibroacoustic_behaviour_of_a_double_glazed_window_modal_testing_and_model_employing_general_para.pdf)]. A pane therefore has *dense* low-Q-spaced modes below the speech band and acts broadly mass-controlled (−12 dB/oct) through most of 200–3800 Hz, rather than 1–3 resonances inside it. Membrane targets (balloon, drum-like film): circular membrane modes are inharmonic, f01 = (2.405/2πa)√(T/σ), overtones at 1.59, 2.14, 2.30, 2.65… × f01 [[HyperPhysics](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/music/cirmem.html)]; the fundamental of a party balloon is set by its tension/size and is not tabulated in a source I could verify (**U**). Lamphone measured a hanging bulb responding mainly at 100–400 Hz with response "weakening as frequency rises" and dropping "dramatically" above 400 Hz through curtain walls [[Lamphone PDF](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/708.pdf)]. The Laser-Meager-Listener study found the *softest* target (Styrofoam cup) the most sensitive [[LML](https://nsaxena.engr.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/238/2023/10/Laser_Meager_Listener_A_Scientific_Exploration_of_Laser-based_Speech_Eavesdropping_in_Commercial_User_Space.pdf)]. | Add a **"stiff plate" branch** to the randomisation: one or more modes **below 250 Hz** (50–200 Hz, Q 10–30) plus a −12 dB/oct roll-off across the band, so the simulator covers glass as well as membranes. Measure the rig target's response with a swept sine or white noise from the speaker (std mic = reference) and read off the modes; that single measurement decides which branch is right. |
| Below f_i the surface "follows the pressure", above it −40 dB/decade. | **S** for a single-degree-of-freedom resonator; the coincidence/plate behaviour of glass is more complex (critical frequency decreases with thickness; coincidence dips 20 dB) [[Marsh, IOA](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/JA%20MARSH%20THE%20AIRBORNE%20SOUND%20INSULATION%20OF%20GLASS.pdf)]. | Keep; note the glass caveat in the report. |

## 2. Optics — knife-edge / photodiode (stage 2)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fraction of a Gaussian spot on the detector vs edge position is I(x) = ½(1+erf((x0+x)/(√2 w))). | **S** | The knife-edge method: transmitted power of a Gaussian beam past an edge is an error function of edge position, P_T(x) ∝ erf(√2 x / r) with r the 1/e² radius [[arXiv physics/0605102](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0605102)]; the Gaussian-profile knife-edge displacement-sensor model agrees with measurement "without model calibration" [[ORNL](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/geometry-based-gaussian-profile-model-optical-knife-edge-displacement-sensor)]. | Keep. |
| Vibration peak amplitude is 0.05–1.2 beam widths ("depth"), alignment offset ±1.2 beam widths. | **A** — numerically generous | Targets move ~1 µm at 57–95 dB SPL [[VisualMic](https://people.csail.mit.edu/mrub/papers/VisualMic_SIGGRAPH2014.pdf)]; a reflected spot on a photodiode is 0.1–1 mm wide, so the *spot* displacement is set by lever arm × 2 × angular tilt, not by the surface displacement itself. Lamphone measured bulb tilt of 0.005–0.06° and a 0.3 mm spot shift over several metres [[Lamphone PDF](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/708.pdf)]. For a flat pane at a few metres with 1 µm displacement, "depth" ≫ 0.05 is unlikely; values near 1 (saturating erf) are optimistic. | Keep the form, but **re-weight depth toward the small-signal (linear) regime**, e.g. log-uniform 0.01–0.5. The erf nonlinearity matters only when depth ≳ 0.5; measure on the rig by comparing the laser-channel harmonic distortion of a loud pure tone with the std mic's. |
| The spot is scaled to its own peak per clip (`xn = x/peak*depth`). | **A** — removes absolute level; the real optical gain is fixed per setup and varies with alignment drift, not per word. | Code reading. | Acceptable for training variety; do **not** interpret sim SNR classes as absolute physical SNR classes. |

## 3. Detector noise (stage 3)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shot-noise σ ∝ √intensity; electronic noise white. | **S** (form) | Photodiode shot noise i = √(2 q I B) for both photocurrent and dark current, plus Johnson noise √(4kTB/R) of the load/feedback resistor; NEP is "the incident light level required to generate a current equal to the noise current" [[Hamamatsu](https://www.hamamatsu.com/eu/en/news/featured-products_and_technologies/2024/photodiodes-exposed-unlocking-the-characteristics-of-these-crucial-sensors.html), [Hamamatsu SNR tool](https://www.hamamatsu.com/us/en/resources/interactive-tools/analog-snr-simulator.html)]. Lamphone's receiver (Thorlabs PDA100A2) was noise-limited by sensor thermal noise, not shot noise [[Lamphone PDF](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/708.pdf)]. | Keep the form. On a low-power laser with a transimpedance amplifier the electronic/Johnson + 1/f term almost certainly dominates; the sim's `shot_frac` 0.2–0.8 is a free parameter. Measure: laser-channel noise spectrum with the beam blocked vs unblocked — if they are equal, shot noise is negligible and `shot_frac` can be ≈0. |
| Noise is **white** across 0–8 kHz, then shaped only by the 2nd-order low-pass. | **A** | TIA noise is typically 1/f at LF plus a rising noise-gain term at HF; the rig's in-band floor (0.013) has not been spectrally resolved. | Measure a quiet-rig spectrum (10 s, 44.1 kHz, Welch) and fit a coloured-noise model; shape the sim noise by that PSD. |
| Noise is referenced to the speech "swing" with SNR ~ U(0, 30) dB over the full band. | **A** — see Q1 below | Rig in-band SNR 3–7.5 dB; the sim's "snr_db" is broadband (0–8 kHz) so the equivalent in-band SNR is ≈ +3.5 dB higher (white noise over 200–3800 Hz carries 45 % of the 0–8 kHz power). | Change the prior (Q1) and define it in-band. |

## 4. Hum and interference (stage 4)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mains hum at 59.9–60.1 Hz with harmonics k = 1..7, amplitudes ∝ U(0.2,1)/k^U(0.7,1.5). | **A** — the *existence* of harmonics is supported, the decay law leaks far too much into the speech band compared with the rig | Hum "almost always includes strong multiples" (120, 180, 240, 300 Hz for 60 Hz) and the fundamental can drift "by up to ~2 %" [[CEDAR](https://cedaraudio.com/article/000005/how-to-remove-hum-from-audio)]; power-supply ripple produces 120 Hz, 180 Hz … [[AV Artifact Atlas](https://www.avartifactatlas.com/artifacts/hum_and_buzz.html)]. Computed here: with the sim's decay law a median **9 % (90th pct 23 %)** of hum power survives the 200–3800 Hz 4th-order zero-phase band-pass, whereas the rig's measured in-band floor bounds the leak at **≤ 0.15 %** of hum power (see derived rig numbers). I.e. the sim's harmonics ≥ 240 Hz are ~20 dB too strong relative to the fundamental. | **Fit the harmonic amplitudes to a measured rig hum spectrum** (one 10-s quiet capture). Until then use k ≤ 3 with ∝ 1/k² or steeper, or keep 7 harmonics but with decay exponent ≥ 2. Keep ±0.1 Hz drift (2 % drift in CEDAR refers to poorly regulated recordings; US grid is far tighter, **U**). |
| Hum level −20…+12 dB relative to speech swing. | **A** — too low | Rig: hum ≈ **+22 dB** above the in-band speech signal (computed). Because `adc()` peak-normalises *after* hum is added, the sim does reproduce hum eating headroom, but only up to 12 dB. | Extend `hum_db` to about −10…+25 dB; most mass at +15…+25 while the rig stays unfixed. |
| Light flicker at 120, 240, 360 Hz, level −40…−5 dB. | **S** (mechanism), level **U** | Fluorescent lamps "flicker at twice the ac mains frequency"; a poor LED driver showed a 120 Hz ripple while good drivers showed none [[EEWorld](https://www.eeworldonline.com/how-to-gauge-led-flicker-faq/)]; Lamphone's pipeline begins with band-stops at the lamp frequency and its harmonics [[Lamphone PDF](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/708.pdf)]. | Keep. On the rig, flicker is indistinguishable from the 120 Hz hum harmonic; the beam-blocked measurement in §3 separates optical flicker from electrical hum. |

## 5. Sampling and anti-aliasing (44.1/48 kHz capture → 8 kHz)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The PCM1808 captures speech cleanly at 44.1/48 kHz. | **S** | TI PCM1808: 24-bit delta-sigma, 64× oversampling, fs 8–96 kHz, decimation filter passband 0.454 fs, stopband 0.583 fs, −65 dB stopband, ±0.05 dB ripple, on-chip HPF −3 dB at 0.91 Hz (48 kHz), DR/SNR 99 dB A-weighted, THD+N −93 dB, full scale 0.6 Vcc p-p = 3 Vp-p at 5 V, 60 kΩ input [[TI SLES177 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/pcm1808)]. At 44.1 kHz the analog anti-alias requirement is only above 25.7 kHz, trivially met. | Keep. The converter is not the bottleneck; its 99 dB DR is ~60 dB better than the rig's in-band SNR. |
| `torchaudio.functional.resample` (sinc_interp_hann, `lowpass_filter_width=6`, `rolloff=0.99`) is an adequate anti-alias filter for 44.1 k/16 k → 8 kHz. | **A** — adequate for the live laser path, marginal for file paths | torchaudio: bandlimited sinc interpolation; `lowpass_filter_width` "Controls the sharpness of the filter"; lower `rolloff` values "reduce anti-aliasing, but also reduce some of the highest frequencies" [[torchaudio docs](https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/2.9.0/generated/torchaudio.functional.resample.html)]. Measured here (44.1 k→8 k, pure tones): 3.5 kHz −1.8 dB, 3.9 kHz −5.3 dB, 4.1 kHz −8.1 dB, **5 kHz alias only −45 dB**, 7 kHz −84 dB; `scipy.signal.resample_poly` with defaults gives −57 dB at 5 kHz. So content between 4 and 5 kHz folds into 3–4 kHz at −8…−45 dB. In the **live laser path** the 200–3800 Hz band-pass runs at the device rate *before* resampling, so it doubles as an anti-alias filter and the issue disappears. In the **file paths** (Speech Commands 16 k→8 k during training; sim 16 k→8 k; saved 16 kHz captures) only torchaudio's filter applies. | Keep for live. For training/eval files consider `lowpass_filter_width=16…64` (or `resample_poly`) — but only if the deployed model is retrained the same way (train/inference resampler must match). Low priority: the aliased band (3–4 kHz) carries little digit energy except "six" (Exp 5). |
| 8 kHz keeps what the laser can deliver. | **S** for this rig | Exp 5: 8 vs 16 kHz differ by +0.35 ± 0.75 pts (not significant); Lamphone and the glass data above show laser-transduced speech is LF-weighted. | Keep 8 kHz (as the PLAN requires). |

| Saved captures preserve level. | **No** — `save_wav` peak-normalises to [−1, 1] and writes **PCM_16** | `src/preprocess.py:save_wav` (`arr = arr/peak`, `subtype="PCM_16"`). ## 7. Filtering (receiver electronics in sim; speech band-pass in code)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sim receiver: 1st-order AC coupling 3–30 Hz, 2nd-order low-pass 3–7.5 kHz, causal `sosfilt`. | **A** | The PCM1808's own HPF is at 0.91 Hz [[TI](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/pcm1808)]; the receiver board's coupling and bandwidth are undocumented (**U**). | Ask hardware for the receiver schematic (coupling capacitor, TIA feedback R/C) and set these two numbers from it. |
| Laser channel band-pass: 4th-order Butterworth 200–3800 Hz, zero-phase (`sosfiltfilt`), applied before the gate and the model. | **S** (filter), **A** (zero-phase in a live loop; see Q4) | Butterworth = maximally flat passband. `filtfilt`: "The combined filter has zero phase and a filter order twice that of the original" [[SciPy filtfilt](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.2.3/reference/generated/scipy.signal.filtfilt.html)]; `sosfiltfilt` is "a forward-backward digital filter using cascaded second-order sections", default `padtype='odd'`, `padlen = 3·(2·n_sections+1)` [[SciPy sosfiltfilt](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.6.0/reference/generated/scipy.signal.sosfiltfilt.html)]. Computed here at 44.1 kHz: causal 4th-order |H| at 60/120/180/240/300 Hz = −43.5/−19/−5.5/−0.8/−0.1 dB; zero-phase (|H|²) = −87/−38/−11/−1.5/−0.2 dB. Causal group delay 3.6 ms at 200 Hz, 0.2 ms at 1 kHz. | Keep the band edges. Decide explicitly between zero-phase-on-a-block (current) and a stateful causal `sosfilt` with `zi` (standard for streaming) and use the *same* choice for fine-tuning data and for the gate calibration, because the two leave different hum residuals (−43 vs −87 dB at 60 Hz). |
| Default `padlen` is adequate on a hum-dominated signal. | **A** | At 44.1 kHz `padlen` = 27 samples = 0.6 ms, versus a 16.7 ms hum period and a 200 Hz corner (settling ~ several ms). The code already skips the last 30 ms of the block for the level estimate, which acknowledges the edge transient. | If zero-phase is kept, pass `padlen ≥ 0.05·fs` (≥ 3 hum cycles) or filter a longer context than is scored. |

## 8. Speech segmentation (energy gate, end-of-word)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy-threshold endpointing (frame energy ≥ 5 % of the clip's peak frame) isolates the digit. | **S** (classic method) | Rabiner & Sambur (1975) endpoint detection of isolated utterances from energy and zero-crossing rate [[Nokia Bell Labs listing](https://nokia.com/bell-labs/publications-and-media/publications/an-algorithm-for-determining-the-endpoints-of-isolated-utterances); [Sheffield MAD demo](https://spandh.dcs.shef.ac.uk/ed_arena/mad/mad/epd/epd.htm)]. Those authors needed the zero-crossing rate precisely because weak fricatives fall below an energy threshold — the failure mode measured below. | Keep for clean data; for the laser the absolute gate is what matters (next rows). |
| Live gate: filtered RMS ≥ 0.02 (laser) / 0.03 (DAQ) over 0.15 s frames; word ends after 0.25 s below gate; words < 0.12 s are clicks; > 1.1 s is forced. | **A** — margins are thin for "six"/"eight" | Measured here on 150 clean clips per digit per corpus (8 kHz, 5 % energy gate): gated duration median/p90 — zero 0.35/0.46 s, one 0.27/0.45, two 0.27/0.35, three 0.27/0.40, four 0.26/0.32, five 0.27/0.35, **six 0.14/0.19 s (FSDD), 0.14/0.48 (SC)**, seven 0.29/0.35, **eight 0.21/0.26**, nine 0.34/0.51 (SC up to 0.42/0.66). Longest *internal* gap: seven median 16–32 ms, p90 64 ms; all other digits p90 ≤ 82 ms. Trailing quiet after the last loud (≥15 %) frame: 16–48 ms median, p90 ≤ 128 ms. Speech Commands clips are 1 s windows chosen by the "Extract Loudest Section" tool, which "tended to center the spoken word" [[Warden 2018 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.03209)]; FSDD clips are "trimmed so that they have near minimal silence" [[TFDS spoken_digit](https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/spoken_digit)]. | 0.25 s end-of-word quiet is safe against "seven"'s internal closure (≤ 64 ms) and could even drop to ~0.15 s to cut latency. The real risk is **WORD_MIN_SEC = 0.12 s vs "six" ≈ 0.14 s**: on the laser the /s/ (≥ 4 kHz, Exp 5) is gone and the vowel is what passes the gate, so "six" and "eight" will sometimes be discarded as clicks. Lower WORD_MIN_SEC to ~0.08 s and measure the per-digit *detection* rate (not just accuracy) on the rig. |
| The 0.15 s level frame + end-of-word trigger keeps the whole word in the window. | **S** | WORD_PAD_SEC 0.25 before/after; measured p90 word length ≤ 0.66 s < 1.0 s window. | Keep. |

## 11. Calibration (temperature scaling, ECE)

| Assumption | S/A/U | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| One temperature fitted on validation NLL gives honest confidences; ECE with 15 bins. | **S** in-distribution | Guo et al. 2017: modern networks "are poorly calibrated" and "temperature scaling — a single-parameter variant of Platt Scaling — is surprisingly effective" [[Guo 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599v1)]; the paper's ECE uses 15 bins (matching `calibration.py`). | Keep. |

## Answers to the five specific questions

**Q1 — Is SNR ~ U(0, 30) dB a reasonable prior given the rig?** No. The rig's in-band SNR is 3–7.5 dB (central ≈ 4.5 dB); the sim's broadband `snr_db` corresponds to about +3.5 dB more in-band, so a sim clip labelled "10 dB" is already cleaner in the speech band than anything the rig has produced, and 83 % of U(0,30) lies above the rig. The 0.013 in-band floor is also *not* white noise from the detector — part of it is hum harmonics (bounded above at 0.15 % of hum power) and part electronic noise, which the sim's SNR knob does not distinguish. Proposed prior, defined **in-band (200–3800 Hz)**: a two-component mixture — 70 % "rig as-is": Normal(5, 3) dB truncated to [−3, 12]; 30 % "rig after the planned fixes" (Exp 6 target amplitude ratio ≥ 5 ≈ 14 dB): Uniform(10, 25) dB. Pinning it down: 50+ paired std/laser utterances from ≥ 3 speakers at the demo distance; per clip, mark speech frames with the std mic (energy gate), compute in-band power in speech vs non-speech frames on the laser channel, SNR = 10·log10((P_speech − P_quiet)/P_quiet); report the median and 10/90 percentiles, then sample `snr_db` from that empirical histogram (the existing `calibrate()` tries to do this but measures the *raw* floor, where hum dominates — band-pass first). Also sample the SNR by *condition* (distance, surface) rather than i.i.d. per clip, so the model does not learn that SNR is independent of everything else.

**Q2 — Is hum as 7 harmonics adequate vs the measured 96 % share?** The harmonic *count* is fine; the *decay law* is not. Mains hum does carry harmonics (CEDAR, AV Artifact Atlas), but with the sim's 1/k^(0.7–1.5) law, a median 9 % (p90 23 %) of the hum power passes the 200–3800 Hz band-pass, while the rig bounds that leak at ≤ 0.15 %. The sim therefore trains the model on in-band buzz at 240–420 Hz that the rig does not have, and under-represents the one thing the rig does have: a single huge 60 Hz line (~22 dB above the in-band speech) that sets the gain/headroom budget. The 96 % figure itself is an amplitude share (power share 99.4 %) and does not say anything about harmonics — only a spectrum does. Recommendation: one 10-s quiet capture → FFT → fit the amplitudes of k = 1..7 (and of 120 Hz optical flicker, by blocking the beam) and replace the random decay with those values ± 6 dB; extend `hum_db` to ≈ +25 dB.

**Q3 — Does the 1–3-mode, 250–3000 Hz surface match plausible targets?** For light, compliant targets (bag, foil, membrane, cup) the picture of a few resonances inside the speech band plus a low-pass trend is supported (Visual Microphone; Laser Meager Listener found Styrofoam the most sensitive; Lamphone's bulb responded mostly at 100–400 Hz). For **window glass** it does not: a modal test of a real window found 29 modes between 30 and ~200 Hz with 1.8–3.2 % damping (Q ≈ 15–28), i.e. the pane is a dense multi-mode plate whose modes sit *below* the sim's 250 Hz floor, with the speech band on the mass-controlled slope and coincidence effects (thickness-dependent, around a few kHz for mm glass — exact numbers not verified here). A balloon or drum-like membrane has inharmonic modes at 1.59, 2.14, 2.30… × f01, with f01 set by tension and radius (HyperPhysics); typical party-balloon fundamentals were not found in a citable source (**unverified**). Recommendation: add a "plate" branch (modes 50–200 Hz, Q 10–30, −12 dB/oct above) and make the branch choice a manifest field; measure the actual target with a swept sine to pick the branch.

**Q4 — Is a 4th-order zero-phase band-pass realistic for live, causal operation?** Not as a *causal* filter: `sosfiltfilt` needs the future samples of the block; in the live code it is applied to a 0.6-s context block on every tick, so the result is only zero-phase in the middle of the block and has an edge transient at the newest samples (the code skips the last 30 ms for that reason; default `padlen` is 0.6 ms at 44.1 kHz, far shorter than one 60 Hz cycle). The consequences: (i) the effective magnitude response is |H|² — −87 dB at 60 Hz, −38 dB at 120 Hz, −11 dB at 180 Hz — versus −43/−19/−5.5 dB for the same filter run causally, so a causal deployment would leave ~20 dB more hum residual and move the 0.02 gate point; (ii) decision latency is ≥ 30 ms (skipped tail) plus the 0.15 s level frame and the 0.25 s end-of-word wait — ~0.4 s word-to-answer, dominated by segmentation, not by the filter; (iii) a causal 4th-order Butterworth would add only 3.6 ms group delay at 200 Hz and 0.2 ms at 1 kHz (computed) — negligible for a whole-word classifier, and no edge effects if run as a stateful `sosfilt(..., zi=...)`. Recommendation: either keep block-zero-phase but use `padlen ≥ 3 hum cycles` and make the fine-tuning data pass through the *same* block filter, or switch to a stateful causal 6th–8th-order Butterworth (to recover the hum rejection) and fine-tune with that. Do not mix: the model's input distribution depends on which one is used.

**Q5 — What does the 0.25 s end-of-word quiet imply for "seven" and "six"?** Measured on 150 clean clips per digit and corpus: the longest internal low-energy gap in "seven" is 16–32 ms median, 64 ms at the 90th percentile — a 0.25 s quiet requirement will never split "seven" (it would take a gap > 250 ms); the parameter could drop to ~0.15 s with no segmentation risk and 100 ms less latency. "Six" is the opposite problem: at 8 kHz with a 5 % energy gate its gated duration is 0.14 s median (0.19 s p90 on FSDD) because the /s/ onset and the trailing /ks/ fall below the gate (11.5 % of the word's energy is above 4 kHz — Exp 5 — and the rest of the fricative is still ~15–20 dB below the vowel). On the laser, with the surface's HF roll-off and an absolute gate at SNR ≈ 5 dB, "six" (and "eight", 0.21 s, whose final /t/ is a silence + burst) is at or below WORD_MIN_SEC = 0.12 s and can be rejected as a click. The trailing fricative itself is handled correctly by the window (WORD_PAD_SEC = 0.25 s after the last active frame covers the ≤ 128 ms p90 trailing quiet), but the model will mostly not see it through the laser, so "six" recognition will rest on the vowel alone. Recommendation: WORD_MIN_SEC → 0.08 s; WORD_END_QUIET_SEC can go to 0.15–0.20 s; track per-digit *detection* rate on the rig as a first-class metric.
