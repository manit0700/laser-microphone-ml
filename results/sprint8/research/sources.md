## Resampling / filtering
- torchaudio.functional.resample docs (2.9.0) — https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/2.9.0/generated/torchaudio.functional.resample.html — bandlimited sinc interpolation; `lowpass_filter_width` (default 6) "controls the sharpness of the filter"; `rolloff` 0.99: "Lower values reduce anti-aliasing, but also reduce some of the highest frequencies"; default method `sinc_interp_hann`.
- SciPy `filtfilt` docs — https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.2.3/reference/generated/scipy.signal.filtfilt.html — "The combined filter has zero phase and a filter order twice that of the original."
- SciPy `sosfiltfilt` docs — https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.6.0/reference/generated/scipy.signal.sosfiltfilt.html — forward-backward SOS filtering; `padtype='odd'` default; `padlen` default 3·(2·n_sections+1) corrected for poles/zeros at origin.
- Measured in this review (scratchpad `checks.py`, torchaudio 2.11.1 / scipy 1.18.1): torchaudio default resample 44.1 k→8 k attenuates a 5 kHz alias by 45 dB, 7 kHz by 84 dB, and is −5.3 dB already at 3.9 kHz; `scipy.signal.resample_poly` defaults: −57 dB at 5 kHz. 4th-order Butterworth 200–3800 Hz at 44.1 kHz: causal |H| −43.5/−19/−5.5/−0.8 dB at 60/120/180/240 Hz; zero-phase squares these; causal group delay 3.6 ms @200 Hz, 0.2 ms @1 kHz.

## Laser-microphone / optical-vibration literature
- Davis, Rubinstein, Wadhwa, Mysore, Durand, Freeman, *The Visual Microphone: Passive Recovery of Sound from Video*, SIGGRAPH 2014 — https://people.csail.mit.edu/mrub/papers/VisualMic_SIGGRAPH2014.pdf — object response modelled as LTI; displacements "in the order of a micrometer" and "approximately linear in Pascals" for 0.1–1 Pa (≈57–95 dB) at 300 Hz; "most objects have a stronger response at lower frequencies", non-monotonic due to "the excitement of vibration modes"; brick gives "almost no signal". Project page: https://people.csail.mit.edu/mrub/VisualMic
- Nassi, Pirutin, Shamir, Elovici, Zadov, *Lamphone: Real-Time Passive Sound Recovery from Light Bulb Vibrations*, IACR ePrint 2020/708 — https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/708.pdf (abstract page https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/708) — Thorlabs PDA100A2 photodiode receiver, NI-9223 16-bit ADC at 2–4 kHz; bulb tilt 0.005–0.06°; response tested 100–400 Hz, weakens with frequency; noise sources: lamp flicker at the lighting frequency (100 Hz) and harmonics, sensor thermal noise, <40 Hz structural vibration; pipeline: band-stops at lamp harmonics, HPF 40 Hz, normalisation, noise gating / MMSE spectral subtraction, equaliser boosting >300 Hz; 24-bit ADC (~1 µV) needed at long range.
- Laser Meager Listener: A Scientific Exploration of Laser-based Speech Eavesdropping in Commercial User Space (Texas A&M, Saxena group) — https://nsaxena.engr.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/238/2023/10/Laser_Meager_Listener_A_Scientific_Exploration_of_Laser-based_Speech_Eavesdropping_in_Commercial_User_Space.pdf — commercial LDVs (Polytec PDV-100, Vibroflex) on cups and a double-pane window; Styrofoam "the most sensitive"; MMSE spectral subtraction (VOICEBOX `ssubmmse`) best; recovered speech STOI 0.20–0.39 vs mic 0.91, SNR 1.2–10 vs 16; human transcription ≤ 65 %, ASR ≤ 9 % — a calibration point for how hard laser speech is even with a $10k+ vibrometer.

## Knife-edge optics
- Quantitative and Qualitative Study of Gaussian Beam Visualization Techniques, arXiv physics/0605102 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0605102 — knife-edge transmitted power P_T(x) ∝ erf(√2 x / r) with r the 1/e² radius; beam radius from the derivative of the knife-edge curve.
- ORNL, Geometry-based Gaussian profile model of an optical knife-edge displacement sensor (Precision Engineering 2022, doi 10.1016/j.precisioneng.2021.10.011) — https://www.ornl.gov/publication/geometry-based-gaussian-profile-model-optical-knife-edge-displacement-sensor — **(abstract only)** Gaussian-profile knife-edge displacement model agrees with measurement "without model calibration" over a linear displacement range.
- CERN Accelerator School, Beam Instrumentation, knife-edge exercise — https://indico.cern.ch/event/1380440/contributions/6204918/attachments/2970079/5226673/CAS_BI_KnifeEdge.pdf — knife-edge scan "records the full beam profile as an error function"; profile recovered by differentiation; refers to Arnaud et al. for the formulas.

## Photodiode noise
- Hamamatsu, *Photodiodes exposed: unlocking the characteristics of these crucial sensors* (2024) — https://www.hamamatsu.com/eu/en/news/featured-products_and_technologies/2024/photodiodes-exposed-unlocking-the-characteristics-of-these-crucial-sensors.html — shot noise of dark current and photocurrent (i = √(2 q I B); the page's text extraction drops the square root), Johnson noise of the shunt resistance, NEP = light level giving current equal to the noise current; linearity lower limit set by NEP.
- Hamamatsu Analog SNR simulator — https://www.hamamatsu.com/us/en/resources/interactive-tools/analog-snr-simulator.html — σ²_shot = 2q(I_S+I_D)·μ·F·Δf; σ²_J = 4k_B T Δf / R_f; SNR ∝ √(light) when shot-noise-limited, ∝ light when readout-limited.
- Thorlabs NEP white paper — https://www.thorlabs.com/images/TabImages/Noise_Equivalent_Power_White_Paper.pdf — **(not fetched: page returned no body)**.

## Surfaces / targets
- S. J. Pietrzko, *Vibroacoustic behaviour of a double glazed window: modal testing and model*, Institute of Acoustics proceedings — https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/sj_pietrzko_vibroacoustic_behaviour_of_a_double_glazed_window_modal_testing_and_model_employing_general_para.pdf — 1480×1230 mm window (9 mm laminated + 6 mm panes); 29 natural frequencies between 30 and ~200 Hz; seven significant modes 94–136 Hz; damping 1.81 % @128.4 Hz, 3.21 % @135.7 Hz; "heavily damped" below 200 Hz.
- J. A. Marsh, *The airborne sound insulation of glass*, IOA — https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/JA%20MARSH%20THE%20AIRBORNE%20SOUND%20INSULATION%20OF%20GLASS.pdf — coincidence (critical) frequency decreases with glass thickness; coincidence dips up to 20 dB at 2 kHz; mass law 6 dB/doubling; no numeric fc or loss factors in the text (the fc ≈ 12 000/t(mm) rule of thumb is therefore **unverified** here).
- HyperPhysics, Circular membrane modes — https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/music/cirmem.html — f01 of an ideal circular membrane depends on tension T (N/m) and areal density σ (kg/m²); modes are inharmonic (overtone ratios 1.59, 2.14, 2.30, 2.65 … appear only as an image on the page, not as text).
- Balloon fundamental frequency: **no citable source found**; left unverified in the review.

## Hum / flicker
- CEDAR Audio, *How to remove hum from audio* — https://cedaraudio.com/article/000005/how-to-remove-hum-from-audio — hum "almost always includes strong multiples" (120, 180, 240, 300 Hz for 60 Hz mains); fundamental can drift "by up to ~2 %"; dimmers produce "rich harmonics".
- AV Artifact Atlas, Hum and buzz — https://www.avartifactatlas.com/artifacts/hum_and_buzz.html — power-supply ripple "is heard as a harmonic of 60 Hz (120 Hz, 180 Hz, etc.)".
- EE World, *How to gauge LED flicker* — https://www.eeworldonline.com/how-to-gauge-led-flicker-faq/ — fluorescent lamps "flicker at twice the ac mains frequency"; a poor LED driver showed 120 Hz ripple; IEEE 1789 flicker limits (≤ 0.08 × frequency % for general use).
- US grid frequency tolerance (the sim's ±0.1 Hz): **unverified**, not searched to a primary source.

## Datasets
- Warden, *Speech Commands: A Dataset for Limited-Vocabulary Speech Recognition*, arXiv 1804.03209 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.03209 (abstract https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03209) — 1-s, 16 kHz, 16-bit mono WAV; crowd-sourced phone/laptop mics; clips chosen by the "Extract Loudest Section" tool which "tended to center the spoken word"; split "chosen using a hash function on its name"; `_background_noise_` folder; v2: 105 829 utterances, 2 618 speakers; 12-class protocol with "Unknown Word" and "Silence"; baseline 88.2 %.
- TensorFlow `speech_commands/input_data.py` (`which_set`) — https://android.googlesource.com/platform/external/tensorflow/+/4380f1c7628/tensorflow/examples/speech_commands/input_data.py — SHA-1 of the filename with everything after `_nohash_` ignored, "to associate particular files as related (for example words spoken by the same person)".
- Free Spoken Digit Dataset (TFDS catalog entry) — https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/spoken_digit — 8 kHz WAV, "trimmed so that they have near minimal silence at the beginnings and ends", 50 recordings per digit per speaker, `{digit}_{speaker}_{index}.wav`. (GitHub repo https://github.com/Jakobovski/free-spoken-digit-dataset — **not fetched**, blocked by allowlist; the repo lists 6 speakers / 3 000 recordings, which matches the 6 × 500 clips in `data/raw`.)

## Speech features / endpointing
- Davis & Mermelstein 1980, *Comparison of parametric representations for monosyllabic word recognition in continuously spoken sentences* — https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/ece417/fa2017/davis80.pdf — 20 triangular mel filters (linear < 1 kHz, log above), 256-pt Hamming window at 10 kHz (25.6 ms), 6.4/12.8 ms spacing, 10 kHz sampling / 5 kHz LPF; 6–10 coefficients suffice; MFCC best of the representations tested.
- Rabiner & Sambur 1975, *An algorithm for determining the endpoints of isolated utterances* (Bell Syst. Tech. J.) — https://nokia.com/bell-labs/publications-and-media/publications/an-algorithm-for-determining-the-endpoints-of-isolated-utterances — **(listing only)**; MAD/Sheffield teaching page on the same algorithm (energy + zero-crossing thresholds) https://spandh.dcs.shef.ac.uk/ed_arena/mad/mad/epd/epd.htm — **(not fetched)**.

## Training / calibration / open-set
- Park et al. 2019, *SpecAugment*, arXiv 1904.08779 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08779v2 — **(abstract only)** applied "directly to the feature inputs (i.e., filter bank coefficients)"; "warping the features, masking blocks of frequency channels, and masking blocks of time steps"; 6.8 % WER LibriSpeech test-other without LM.
- Müller, Kornblith, Hinton 2019, *When does label smoothing help?*, arXiv 1906.02629 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02629v2 — **(abstract only)** label smoothing improves calibration; "results in loss of information in the logits about resemblances between instances of different classes"; hurts distillation.
- Guo, Pleiss, Sun, Weinberger 2017, *On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks*, arXiv 1706.04599 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599v1 — **(abstract only)** "modern neural networks … are poorly calibrated"; "temperature scaling — a single-parameter variant of Platt Scaling — is surprisingly effective".
- Ovadia et al. 2019, *Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty? Evaluating Predictive Uncertainty Under Dataset Shift*, arXiv 1906.02530 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530 — **(abstract only)** under dataset shift "traditional post-hoc calibration does indeed fall short"; methods that marginalise over models do better.
- Hendrycks & Gimpel 2017, *A Baseline for Detecting Misclassified and Out-of-Distribution Examples in Neural Networks*, arXiv 1610.02136 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02136v3 — **(abstract only)** "Correctly classified examples tend to have greater maximum softmax probabilities"; the baseline "can sometimes be surpassed".
- Geng, Huang, Chen 2020, *Recent Advances in Open Set Recognition: A Survey*, arXiv 1811.08581 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08581v2 — **(abstract only)** open-set recognition: "unknown classes can be submitted to an algorithm during testing"; classifiers must "accurately classify the seen classes" and "effectively deal with the unseen ones". Scheirer et al. 2013 (*Toward Open Set Recognition*, IEEE TPAMI) is the origin of the term — **not fetched**.
